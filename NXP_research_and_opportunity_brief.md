## 2. Strategic moves that create consulting demand

1. **Hybrid manufacturing and 300mm consolidation.** NXP is moving its internal fabs to 300mm and exiting 200mm completely over about ten years, with Nijmegen likely closing by the late 2030s.
   - VSMC (Singapore): NXP owns 40% and is entitled to 40% of capacity. Grand opening was 28 Sep 2026, with volume production planned for Q1 2027.
   - ESMC (Dresden): NXP owns 10% and is entitled to 10% of capacity. Initial production targeted for 2028.
   - Source discrepancy: one report suggests the VSMC 2029 output target was reduced, while NXP says its planned capacity is unchanged. Unresolved.
   - Nokia agreed to buy NXP's Chandler, Arizona fab campus. NXP was already scheduled to close it in 2027, and the deal is expected to close in Q1 2029.
   - This means foundry-partner and joint-venture capacity sits alongside a shrinking internal footprint. Qualification, transfer and allocation across sites are all in play.
2. **Back-end expansion.** The Petaling Jaya, Malaysia assembly/test expansion is a stated part of the hybrid model, for flexibility, supply control and geographic diversification.
3. **Portfolio and M&A integration.** TTTech Auto (closed), Kinara (reported ~$307M) and Aviva Links all closed by the Q3 2025 call. Management expects material revenue impact in 2028 and beyond, so integration work runs well ahead of the revenue.
4. **Inventory and channel normalization.** Elevated days of inventory, channel below target, and growing backlog at distributors. Management is weighing selective channel build against margin.
5. **Pricing and supply tightness.** Selective pricing, input-cost inflation and lengthening lead times mark a shift from the 2024–25 inventory correction toward an allocation-sensitive upcycle.
6. **Physical AI and data center.** New growth vectors (the $1.5B funnel and >$500M data-center revenue) add different customers, longer design cycles and different forecast behavior than the core automotive base.
7. **Leadership transition.** A new CEO since Oct 2025 and a "temporary" proxy label suggest a strategy and operating-model review may be live. Treat as a signal to test, not a fact.

## 4. Gaps and what Bristlecone's contacts must supply

1. Who the CIO / head of IT and the head of supply chain planning are. Only the Chief Operations & Manufacturing Officer was found.
2. The current ERP and planning stack. SAP is likely but unconfirmed, and the IBP and Kinaxis questions are open.
3. 2026 inventory days and channel weeks from the Q1/Q2 2026 filings (the figures above are from 2025).
4. The CEO status: whether "temporary" in the proxy reflects a real search or just a drafting label, and whether a strategy or operating-model review is under way.
5. Which consultancies and SIs are already embedded.
6. How NXP compares with peers (TI, Infineon, ADI, onsemi, Microchip, STMicro) on operations strategy. See the ADI brief in this repo for the ADI side.
7. A source conflict to close out: VSMC output target (reduced or unchanged).
